Privacy policy

SABRE Plus collects nothing about you.

Last updated 2026-08-04

SABRE Plus is a free, open-source Highway Radar plugin. It has no servers, no accounts and no analytics, and it collects no personal data. Everything it does runs on your own device. This page sets out exactly what stays on your device, what leaves it, and what never happens.

The short version

  • The app runs entirely on your phone. This project runs no server for your data to reach.
  • No analytics, crash-reporting services, advertising or trackers of any kind.
  • No accounts, no user IDs, and no reading of your device's advertising ID.
  • No location history is stored, and nothing is ever sold or shared.

What is stored on your device

All of this stays in the app's private storage, and the app never transmits it:

  • Your settings. Which sources and categories are on, the age filter, the wildfire size limit, and so on.
  • An anonymous Waze session. To use the Waze feature the app registers an anonymous session with Waze, with no name, email, phone or account. A session token is cached so it does not have to register again every time.
  • A short diagnostics log. A small in-memory record of recent activity (counts, category names, source health and errors) to help with troubleshooting. It goes somewhere only if you tap Share diagnostics and choose where to send it. It contains no location, street names, alert IDs or personal data.
  • A local crash log. If the app crashes, it writes a summary to a private file so you can include it in a bug report if you want to. It stays on your device unless you share it.

What leaves your device

To show you road conditions, the app fetches data from public and third-party sources. Those requests go straight from your device to those services. They do not pass through any server this project runs.

waze.com		 Nearby crowd-sourced alerts Yes. To return alerts near you, the app sends your approximate location, a map area around you, over an anonymous session.
GitHub

As with any app that uses the internet, each service you contact can see your device's public IP address, and what they do with it is governed by their own privacy policies. Waze is the only case where your approximate location leaves the device. If you would rather send no location to Waze, turn the Waze source off in Highway Radar's settings.

What never happens

  • No analytics or usage tracking.
  • No advertising or ad identifiers.
  • No accounts, sign-ins or user identifiers.
  • No location history and no profiles.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of data with anyone.

Permissions

The app asks only for what it needs to relay alerts while you drive: internet access, a foreground-service notification, a wake lock, exact alarms to keep the service reachable, and an optional battery-optimization exemption. It does not ask for your contacts, camera, microphone, photos or precise device location. The location it filters by comes from Highway Radar's request, not from a location permission of its own.

Children

This app is a navigation utility for drivers and is not directed at children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the update is committed to PRIVACY.md in the public repository and the date at the top of this page changes with it.
